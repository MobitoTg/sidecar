Sidecar

A personal AI chief of staff. One voice-controlled orchestrator, a team of specialist agents, and a fleet of Linux machines that work as one. She's built from Claude Code, a handful of small scripts and plain conversation, and this is what we built together in about a day.

How it works

I only ever talk to the chief of staff. She decides whether to do a job herself or hand it to a specialist, writes a full brief for that agent, checks what comes back, and reports to me. I hear her answers out loud. Real projects run through the full team: product frames the problem, the project manager plans it, the tech lead designs it, the engineer builds it, and the tech lead reviews it.

Me (voice or keyboard)→ Chief of staff→ Specialist agents→ Work gets checked→ Spoken answer

Design agent

Designs the look and layout of pages and apps. Its first job was a full redesign of a barbershop demo site: a new type and color system, a price board and a booking card, all passing WCAG AA contrast checks.

Shopping agent

Finds the best total price across new, open-box, used and refurbished sellers, vets each seller, and returns a ranked shortlist. It only researches and never buys.

research

Private specialists

Research agents with strict evidence rules: every claim cites a source and a date, and guru or self-reported numbers never count as proof. The chief of staff checks their sources before I see anything.

Hands-free voice

I click a mic in the top bar and just talk, from any workspace, while doing anything else. Speech-to-text and the voice both run locally, so no audio leaves the machine.

Listens for speech

A small voice-activity detector notices when I start talking and treats a 1.5-second pause as the end of a message.

Transcribes locally

Whisper runs on the laptop through Voxtype. Saying "wait" at the end holds a thought open until I finish my next sentence.

Delivers anywhere

The chief of staff lives in a tmux session, and my words are typed straight into it. No windows jump around, and I can be on any workspace.

Talks back

When she finishes, an offline Piper voice reads the opening of her reply aloud. The mic is ignored while she speaks, and a new message cuts her off mid-sentence.

Ready: go ahead and talk Working or speaking Not running

A custom Omarchy bar module puts three controls next to the clock: open the chief of staff, a live status light, and the mic toggle.

Any number of machines, one assistant

Sidecar isn't tied to one computer. Every machine runs the same chief of staff from one shared package, every copy shares one memory, and any machine can reach any other. It started with a laptop and a desktop, and it's built to grow to four or more: laptops as sidecars, desktops as workhorses and storage.

Shared kit + memory (one hub)⇄ Laptop 1Laptop 2 Desktop 1Desktop 2…machine n

Remote power

She can sleep or wake any desktop over the network. Sleep instead of shutdown keeps the encrypted disk unlocked, so a wake takes about 18 seconds with no password screen.

Files on tap

Any machine's drive can show up as a normal folder on the others, so files live on the machine with the most space and are reachable from everywhere.

Any machine to any machine

Every machine can reach every other over SSH, with keys only. Laptops run SSH only on the home network and switch it off anywhere else. Desktops stay reachable and wake on demand.

The browser too

Through the Claude in Chrome extension, she can drive websites on any connected machine, like playing a song or opening an email, and asks before anything irreversible.

Same context everywhere

A short handoff brief and the shared memory load at the start of every session, on every machine. Start talking to her on the laptop in one room, pick it up on the desktop in another, and she already knows what's in flight, what's next and what's waiting on me.

Add a machine in minutes

Agents, scripts, the bar module and memory live in one git repo. Each machine syncs with a central hub every five minutes. Adding machine n means one install script and one key exchange, not reconfiguring every other machine.

Guardrails

Getting the trust model right mattered more than any single feature.

  • The assistant never sees passwords. Whenever something needs one, she opens a terminal and I type it there myself.
  • Ask first. Purchases, sending messages, publishing and deleting all need my explicit OK every time, and an OK for one action doesn't carry over to the next.
  • Agents are research-only. No accounts, no posting, no spending. The chief of staff checks their sources before I see their results.
  • Web pages are data, not instructions. Text on a page can't tell any agent what to do.
  • The safety layer pushes back. Claude Code's own checks stopped her twice while she was setting up an always-on network service, until I explicitly approved it.
